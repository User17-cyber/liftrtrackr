# Product

## Register

product

## Users
One person (and anyone they share it with) logging their own meals, almost always on an iPhone at the table: photo taken, numbers checked, meal logged in under a minute, often one-handed. Desktop is a secondary surface for reviewing past days and setting up the profile. Each viewer has a private log and their own maintenance estimate.

## Product Purpose
LiftrTrackr turns a meal photo into a calorie and macronutrient entry whose numbers come from a credible source (USDA FoodData Central), not from model guesswork. Claude identifies foods and estimates grams; USDA supplies every nutrient value; validated equations (Mifflin-St Jeor, with Harris-Benedict and Katch-McArdle for comparison) estimate maintenance calories. A long-term weight log smooths daily weigh-ins into a trend and, combined with logged meals, measures the user's real maintenance by energy balance. Success: the user trusts the number enough to log it without second-guessing, can correct it in seconds when the estimate is off, and sees their true maintenance emerge from their own data over weeks.

## Brand Personality
Precise, trustworthy, calm. A well-made instrument, not a coach. The voice states facts plainly and shows its sources. Heavy ledger rules and plain, well-set figures carry the identity. The product does not imitate regulatory artwork such as the Nutrition Facts label (removed at the user's request); totals are shown in its own calm panel style.

## Anti-references
- Cluttered trackers (MyFitnessPal-style): tiny text, every number on screen at once, ad-like density.
- Guilt and diet culture: red alarm states, shaming copy, "you failed" framing when over target. Over target is information, not a warning siren.
- Generic SaaS dashboards: card grids, stat tiles, gradient accents, hero metrics.
- Gamification (not ruled out by the user, but off-brand for an instrument): streaks, badges, confetti.

## Design Principles
1. Show the source. Every number can be traced to a USDA entry or a named equation; provenance is visible, not buried.
2. Phone first, one hand. The primary flow (photo → review → log) fits an iPhone screen with thumb-reachable actions and no horizontal scroll.
3. Correction is cheap. Grams, portions and matches are editable in place; the estimate is a starting point.
4. Neutral about outcomes. Being over or under target is reported in the same calm register.
5. Quiet until needed. Detail (equations, per-food macros, sources) is available on demand, not on screen by default.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
- WCAG 2.2 AA: 4.5:1 body contrast (including placeholders and muted text), visible focus, full keyboard and screen-reader support, reduced motion respected.
- Larger text: base body size ≥16px on phones (also prevents iOS input zoom), tap targets ≥44×44px.
- Color-blind safe: protein, carbohydrate and fat are distinguishable without hue (labels, initials or position always accompany color).
